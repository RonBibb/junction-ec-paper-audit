# Ingoing Eddington–Finkelstein comparison

Introduce

\[
v=T+r_*(R),\qquad \frac{dr_*}{dR}=\frac1F.
\]

The parent metric becomes

\[
ds^2=-Fdv^2+2\,dv\,dR+R^2d\Omega^2.
\]

For (X_P^\mu=(v(\tau),R(\tau),\theta,\phi)), proper-time normalization is

\[
-F\dot v^2+2\dot v\dot R=-1.
\]

The future-ingoing root is

\[
\dot v=\frac{\beta+\dot R}{F}
=\frac1{\beta-\dot R}.
\]

The normal covector is

\[
n_{\mu,P}=\epsilon_P(-\dot R,\dot v,0,0).
\]

It is exactly orthogonal to the shell tangent and has unit spacelike norm. Its radial
contravariant component is

\[
n^R=\epsilon_P(-\dot R+F\dot v)=\epsilon_P\beta.
\]

The complete calculation gives

\[
K^\theta{}_{\theta,P}=\epsilon_P\frac{\beta}{R},
\]

\[
K^\tau{}_{\tau,P}
=\epsilon_P\frac{\ddot R+m/R^2}{\beta},
\]

identically matching the Schwarzschild-coordinate expressions for (F>0\). Because the child
sector is unchanged, \(\sigma\) and \(p_s\) also agree.

For an infalling timelike shell at the horizon, \(F\to0\), \(\dot R<0\), and
\(\beta\to-\dot R\). Thus

\[
\dot v\to-\frac1{2\dot R},
\]

which is finite. Both parent extrinsic-curvature components remain finite for finite shell
acceleration and nonzero \(\dot R\). A timelike shell cannot have \(\dot R=0\) at the horizon,
because the EF normalization would read (2\dot v\dot R=-1\).

At an exterior turning point \(\dot R=0\), (H_B=0\) and

\[
K^\theta{}_{\theta,C1}=0,
\qquad
K^\theta{}_{\theta,P}=\epsilon_P\frac{\sqrt F}{R}.
\]

The surface density remains finite and negative for the ordinary orientation. The pressure
is finite for finite \(\ddot R,\dot X,H_A,X\).

The divergence of \(dT/d\tau=\beta/F\) at the horizon is a Schwarzschild-coordinate
singularity. It is not reversed time, a reflection, or a time-compression law.
